# Write the policy for the tools people already use

At my previous employer, we tried to ban AI tools. It lasted about a fortnight.

## Bans move the risk, they do not remove it

When a company blocks AI tools, the work goes underground, not away. Teams delve into personal accounts, and the security landscape gets harder to see. That is a choice, not a fix.

## The gap is the policy, not the tools

Most people want rules, not punishment. They want an approved option, not a lecture. Without one, they weave a tapestry of workarounds that nobody can audit.

## Govern what is already happening

Start with visibility, not enforcement. List the tools in use, decide which data may go into them, and give people an approved option for each common task. A ban is a policy for the tools you can see.

What did your organisation do first: block, or write the rules?
