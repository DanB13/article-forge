# Write the policy for the tools people already use

I tried to ban AI tools once. It took eleven days for the workarounds to outnumber the rules.

## Bans move the risk; they do not remove it

A survey of 2,000 people by Acme Research found that 61% of office workers use generative AI tools at least weekly. Block those tools and the work does not stop. It moves to personal phones, where nobody can see it.

## The gap is the policy

38% of people have used an AI tool their employer has not approved, and only 29% say their employer has a written policy. That is the exposure: use without rules.

## Govern what is already happening

List the tools in use. Decide which data may go into them. Give people an approved option for each common task. A ban is a policy for the tools you can see.
